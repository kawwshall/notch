Wordmark

A single rank chevron set against the name in Erode. "Notch" is read as the idiom — top-notch, taking it up a notch — so the mark rises. The chevron is the insignia sense of a notch: rank earned, a stripe on a sleeve. That suits a product built for people whose job is making somebody better.

The mark is drawn in currentColor, so it inherits ink on paper and paper on ink without a second asset.

Construction

Mark height is 0.66× the type size, and width tracks height at the viewBox's 24:20. Never scale the two independently — the apex skews off centre. A mark set to the full em box reads oversized beside the letters, because unlike a letter it has no ascender or descender to absorb the extra height.

Rejected — reads as a UI caret

Shallow 3:2 on a light stroke. This is the shape of an expand/collapse control, so the logo reads as something to click rather than something stamped.

Steeper 6:5 at stroke 3.8, mitred apex, cut ends rather than rounded. Heavier than this outweighs Erode's stems and the lockup comes apart.

Mark and device are different things — on purpose

The chevron is the brand: what the product is for — getting better, staying top-notch. The tally is the interface device: what the product does — counting what's left. Aspiration on the outside, mechanics on the inside. Don't put tally marks in the logo, and don't put chevrons in the ledger.

Voice

Notch talks the way a good coach does: plainly, without selling. The user is a working professional, not a "user", and their clients are people with names. No exclamation marks, no growth language, no emoji anywhere in the product.

Layout rules

  • Rules, never shadows. Hairlines at --rule separate everything.
  • Corners are 3px. Nothing in this product is a pill.
  • Asymmetric grids on marketing pages; no three-across card rows.
  • No icons. If something needs an icon to be understood, rewrite the label.
  • Section headers are .label over a rule, not a type step.
